# UI review mode and density audit

## Categories

A. Information density  B. Information hierarchy  C. Operational efficiency  D. Tables  E. Forms  F. Navigation  G. Actions  H. Visual noise  I. Spacing  J. Typography  K. Status semantics  L. Keyboard  M. Touch  N. Responsive behavior  O. Accessibility  P. Performance.

## Procedure

1. Identify operator, primary task, device, data volume, and failure cost.
2. Identify required information and classify PRIMARY / SECONDARY / TERTIARY.
3. Identify unnecessary vertical/horizontal space and decorative chrome.
4. Inspect hierarchy, tables/forms/layout, action cost, keyboard, touch, responsiveness, accessibility, status, and performance.
5. Recommend specific changes with evidence and operational impact.
6. Prioritize by severity and effort.

Severity: **CRITICAL** blocks completion, causes unsafe/irreversible action, or makes operation inaccessible; **HIGH** materially increases time, errors, or missed exceptions; **MEDIUM** creates repeated friction or inconsistency; **LOW** is polish with limited operational effect.

Prefer: “The operator must open every asset to see Store and Status, though both are needed for routine comparison.” Avoid: “This does not look modern.”

## Operational efficiency audit

1. State the operator goal and success result.
2. Identify information required before the decision.
3. Identify the action and expected feedback.
4. Inspect unnecessary transitions, modals, repeated input, scrolling, focus changes, and context resets.
5. Identify workflow/state/permission/safety constraints.
6. Test repeated operation and next-item behavior.
7. Inspect partial failure, stale data, long-running work, history, and recovery.
8. Recommend changes with severity and operational impact.

A valid finding explains how the workflow distance or risk changes. “More modern” is not a finding.
